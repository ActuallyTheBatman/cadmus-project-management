DVBE Participation on CPUC-Regulated Programs: What's Required

Programs regulated by the California Public Utilities Commission — including telecommunications and utility-adjacent public-interest programs — frequently carry their own DVBE participation expectations. Here's what that typically looks like for a prime building a proposal.

Why CPUC-adjacent programs often require DVBE participation

Many CPUC-regulated and CPUC-adjacent public-interest programs are funded or overseen in a way that ties back to state supplier-diversity policy. A prime bidding on this type of program should expect the RFP to specify a DVBE participation commitment and documentation package as a condition of the proposal, not an optional add-on.

What's typically required

  • A minimum DVBE participation commitment stated in the solicitation — commonly in the range of a few percent of contract value, though the exact figure is set per RFP
  • A signed Bidder Declaration confirming DVBE participation
  • A DVBE Declaration (California Std. Form 843 / PD 843)
  • A Commercially Useful Function (CUF) Certification describing the DVBE's specific, real scope of work
  • If the DVBE is providing named personnel — such as a Key Staff Project Manager — Key Staff Qualifications and reference forms documenting that individual's relevant experience

Key Staff roles are common, not just line-item participation

On project management and PMO-heavy solicitations, it's common for the DVBE's participation to take the form of a named Key Staff role — for example, a DVBE-certified Project Manager serving as the proposal's designated project lead — rather than a smaller supporting line item. When that's the structure, the proposal package typically needs to document that individual's qualifications and professional references in addition to the standard DVBE forms, since the evaluator is assessing both the DVBE commitment and the named person's ability to perform the role.

Cadmus has supported this exact structure — proposing a PMP-certified, DVBE-affiliated Project Manager as named Key Staff on a CPUC-adjacent program proposal, including the supporting qualifications forms, reference forms, and DVBE/CUF documentation package.

Frequently asked questions

Does the CPUC require DVBE participation on its programs?

CPUC-regulated programs and the contracts supporting them frequently include DVBE and broader supplier-diversity requirements, though the specific threshold and documentation vary by program and solicitation — always confirm against the individual RFP.

What documentation is usually required to prove DVBE participation?

Typically a Bidder Declaration, a DVBE Declaration (STD.843/PD 843), a CUF Certification describing the DVBE's role, and — when the DVBE is providing named personnel — Key Staff qualifications and reference forms.
