DVBE vs. SDVOSB vs. SB Micro: What's the Difference?

Primes and agencies building a bid team often see these three terms side by side and treat them as interchangeable. They aren't. Here's what each one actually certifies, who administers it, and when it matters.

The quick comparison

CertificationWhat it certifiesWho administers itTypically used for
DVBE Disabled Veteran Business Enterprise — a business at least 51% owned and controlled by one or more disabled veterans California Department of General Services (DGS) California state and public-agency RFP participation goals and incentives
SDVOSB Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business — a small business at least 51% owned and controlled by one or more service-disabled veterans U.S. Small Business Administration / federal agencies Federal set-aside contracts and solicitations
SB Micro California certified Small Business with Microbusiness designation — a small business meeting California's microbusiness revenue/size threshold California Department of General Services (DGS) California small-business set-asides, simplified bidding thresholds, and additional procurement preferences

Why the distinction matters

DVBE and SDVOSB both relate to veteran ownership, but they live in different procurement systems. DVBE is a California-specific certification that counts toward state and public-agency DVBE participation goals. SDVOSB is a federal designation that qualifies a firm for federal set-aside contracts administered by agencies like the Defense Logistics Agency. A firm can hold both — many do — but satisfying a state RFP's DVBE requirement and qualifying for a federal SDVOSB set-aside are two separate processes with separate paperwork.

SB Micro is a different axis entirely: it's about business size, not veteran ownership. California's microbusiness designation sits inside the small-business certification and can unlock additional procurement advantages — including, in some cases, simplified or expedited bidding thresholds — independent of DVBE or SDVOSB status.

How they combine

A firm can hold all three at once, and when it does, each one does different work on a given bid:

On a CA state RFP

DVBE and SB Micro status can both contribute toward small-business and DVBE participation incentives, depending on the solicitation's scoring.

On a federal RFQ/RFP

SDVOSB status is what qualifies the firm for a federal set-aside — DVBE and SB Micro don't apply at the federal level.

On a prime's teaming decision

A prime assembling a bid team may want all three represented, since each credential answers a different compliance requirement.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between DVBE and SDVOSB?

DVBE is a California state certification used for state and public-agency procurement; SDVOSB is a federal designation used for federal set-aside contracts. A firm can hold both, but they're separate certifications tied to separate procurement systems.

Can a firm be both SB Micro and DVBE?

Yes — they're separate, non-exclusive certifications. Many California small businesses hold both, and solicitations may recognize them independently or in combination.

Which certification matters most for a given RFP?

It depends entirely on the solicitation. State and public-agency RFPs typically reference DVBE and small-business/microbusiness goals; federal RFPs typically reference SDVOSB set-asides. Read the specific solicitation's certification requirements rather than assuming.
