CMAS vs. Direct Award: How California Agencies Procure PM Services

If you're an agency buyer or a prime weighing how to bring in project management support, the procurement path you choose changes your timeline, documentation burden, and vendor pool. Here's how CMAS compares to direct award and a competitive RFP.

The three paths, briefly

PathHow it worksTypical timelineBest fit
CMAS Agency purchases directly from a vendor already holding a California Multiple Award Schedule contract, under pre-negotiated terms Days to a few weeks Known scope, purchasing authority threshold met, need to move quickly
Direct Award / Sole Source Agency awards directly to a vendor without a competitive process, typically justified by urgency, uniqueness, or a documented sole-source rationale Days to weeks, plus justification documentation Narrow, well-justified circumstances; generally more scrutinized than CMAS
Competitive RFP Full competitive solicitation with published requirements, proposal submissions, evaluation, and award Months Larger, longer-term, or higher-value engagements; when a broad vendor pool and best-value evaluation are wanted

Why CMAS exists

CMAS lets the state pre-vet a vendor's pricing, qualifications, and terms once, so individual agencies don't have to run a full competitive procurement every time they need that same category of service. For project management and PMO support specifically, that means an agency with a CMAS-qualified PM vendor already available can engage that vendor directly, within CMAS purchasing authority limits, instead of spending months on an RFP for work that may need to start sooner.

Where DVBE participation fits in

A vendor can hold a CMAS contract and DVBE certification at the same time. For an agency that wants both an expedited procurement path and DVBE participation credit, a DVBE-certified CMAS holder can satisfy both goals through a single, direct purchase — without the agency having to separately structure DVBE participation into a longer competitive RFP.

Frequently asked questions

What is CMAS?

CMAS, the California Multiple Award Schedule, is a pre-negotiated state contract vehicle that lets California public agencies purchase goods and services from an approved vendor without running a separate competitive bid.

When does an agency use CMAS instead of a competitive RFP?

Typically when the agency wants to move faster than a full RFP allows, the purchase falls within CMAS purchasing authority thresholds, and a qualified CMAS contract holder already offers the needed service.

Can CMAS and DVBE participation work together?

Yes — a vendor can hold both, which can make it a straightforward option for an agency that wants expedited procurement and DVBE participation credit in the same transaction.
