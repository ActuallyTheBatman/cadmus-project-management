What Is Commercially Useful Function (CUF) for DVBE Subcontracts?

If you're a prime contractor adding a DVBE partner to satisfy a California RFP's participation requirement, "commercially useful function" is the term that decides whether that participation actually counts.

The short version

Commercially Useful Function (CUF) means the DVBE firm has to do real work on the contract — not just appear on a subcontractor list. The DVBE must perform, manage, or supervise a distinct scope of work using its own business services and capabilities, and be responsible for the quality, timeliness, and delivery of that work. A DVBE listed only to satisfy a participation percentage, with no actual role, does not meet CUF and can put a bid's DVBE credit at risk.

What a compliant CUF role typically looks like

For project management and PMO-type participation, a defensible CUF role usually includes a defined, documented scope such as:

  • Project and contract management support tied to specific deliverables
  • Delivery governance, schedule and milestone tracking
  • Risk and issue management and action-item follow-up
  • Stakeholder coordination and status reporting
  • PMO documentation for assigned project-based tasks

The DVBE performs, manages, and supervises its own portion of the work, and coordinates with the prime and the awarding agency as required — rather than the prime performing the work and simply routing payment through the DVBE.

Forms and documentation that typically come with it

On California state and public-agency RFPs, DVBE participation is usually backed by a documented package, which can include:

  • A Bidder Declaration confirming DVBE participation
  • A Bidding Preferences/Incentives form
  • A DVBE Declaration (STD.843 / PD 843)
  • A CUF Certification describing the DVBE's specific role
  • A minimum DVBE participation commitment — often in the range of 3 percent, though this varies by solicitation

How Cadmus typically frames its CUF role

"[Cadmus] will perform a commercially useful function by providing project and contract management support... Cadmus will be responsible for a distinct element of the work, including project coordination, delivery governance, schedule and milestone tracking, risk and issue management, action-item follow-up, stakeholder coordination, status reporting, contract/task-order support, and PMO-related documentation for assigned project-based tasks. Cadmus will perform, manage, and supervise its own project management work using its normal business services and capabilities."

Why this matters for primes

A DVBE participation commitment that doesn't hold up to a CUF review can jeopardize the points or incentive credit a bid was counting on — and can draw scrutiny after award. Structuring the DVBE's role around a real, defined, documentable scope from the start is the difference between participation that survives review and participation that doesn't.

Frequently asked questions

Does the DVBE have to do the work itself, or can it be subcontracted further?

CUF generally requires the DVBE to actually perform, manage, or supervise its scope using its own capabilities — not pass the work through to another firm while collecting a fee.

What percentage of DVBE participation is usually required?

It varies by solicitation. A minimum commitment in the 3 percent range is common on California state RFPs, but the exact figure, forms, and documentation are set by each individual solicitation.

Can project management or PMO support count as CUF?

Yes, when the role is a distinct, defined scope — such as schedule tracking, risk/issue management, reporting, and delivery governance — that the DVBE actually performs and is accountable for, rather than a nominal listing.
